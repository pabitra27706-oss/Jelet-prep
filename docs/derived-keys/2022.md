Both disputes from the first pass are **withdrawn**, and both for the same
reason: my derivation was wrong, not the paper.

- **Q40** was called a defect because a mis-integration gave x = y(1−ln y) and
  y(−3) ≈ 4.97. The equation is separable as d(x/y) = −dy, giving x = 2y − y²,
  so y(−3) = 3 **or** −1. Q40 is Category-II, so both (A) and (C) are correct.
- **Q59** was called a defect because the doubling convention
  (1/F = 2/f_lens + 2/f_liquid) gives 12.5 cm at μ = 1.6, which matches no option.
  The printed 25 cm is reproduced exactly by the single-pass form
  1/F = 1/f_lens + 1/f_liquid = (2−μ)/10, which gives μ = 1.6, option (C).

No question in this paper now needs to be marked unscoreable.

## Where it is still weak

- **Eighteen entries are provisional.** These are the questions where a second
  or third solver disagreed and the page settled it — a real misreading of a
  figure, in most cases. They are keyed from the printed page and the reasoning
  is recorded, but they are the first place a human should look.
- **Six figure questions** (Q44, Q49, Q55, Q57, Q62, Q69) were answered from page
  images. They are scoreable on the strength of the text but still need a redrawn
  figure beside them (ADR-0002) before the site can present them properly.
- **Q48 has a printing defect**: all four options omit the square root that the
  physics requires. (D) is recorded as the intended answer, with the defect noted.
